Give a song 20 s to resolve, hedge a stalled /player, and run yt-dlp one at a time (B9)

When YouTube stalled, a song could sit on "Resolving source..." for well
over half a minute before the player said it could not play it and moved
on: 8 s for /player, a retry 1.2 s later with 8 s more, then 12 s each for
yt-dlp and the muxed stream (41 s measured with everything dark, ~49 s
with a visitor id to fetch first). Qt's limits count only silence, and
the retry went out on the same, possibly stalled, HTTP/2 connection.
Meanwhile a song fetched ahead of time could hold a yt-dlp lookup while
the song being waited for started a second one beside it, and a download
starting at that moment made three.

- A song someone is waiting for has 20 s from being asked for to its
  answer, JioSaavn's part included. Past it the walk stops, failed() says
  what was tried, and the player skips on as for any song that will not
  resolve. A YouTube answer held for JioSaavn plays instead.
- /player has 3 s for the whole call (visitor id, second client and a
  renewed id included). A first request with no answer at 1.2 s, or one
  that fails before then, is sent once more beside it through a network
  manager made for it, so it gets a connection of its own; the first
  answer wins and the other is dropped. One hedge a call, none for the
  second client. yt-dlp now starts at ~3 s instead of ~17 s.
- One yt-dlp resolve at a time (the sound's and the muxed stream's). The
  song being waited for goes first: a prefetch's lookup is stopped for it
  and starts again after, still on its rung. Each rung's 12 s counts from
  when its lookup starts. No new download starts while such a song
  resolves; running ones carry on.
- Switches back: youtube.player_deadline=off,
  playback.resolve_deadline=off, ytdlp.resolves=parallel. The log says
  when each yt-dlp lookup starts, waits its turn or is stopped.
- New --bounds-test [rounds] [--before]: 22 checks against a stand-in
  YouTube, with yt-dlp real or sent through a proxy that never answers,
  and the real mpv for the skip notice; --before times the same with
  every switch back.

Measured on the Release build, before -> after (median / p90):
- --bounds-test 6 --before, every /player delayed 10 s: yt-dlp started
  at 17,192 / 17,206 -> 3,002 / 3,008 ms; the song answered at
  19,697 / 19,901 -> 5,388 / 5,631 ms (n=6 each). Plan target ~6 s: met.
- First /player stalled, a second connection fine: answered at 1,231 ms.
- YouTube and yt-dlp both dark: the skip notice 41,292 -> 20,011 ms
  after Play (n=1 each). That is the 20 s limit itself plus 11 ms of
  timer and handling, so "within 20 s" holds only to that margin.
- bench_A_play.ps1, 12 ids, the B8 and B9 releases interleaved over 4
  rounds (2 in each order), n=48 per arm: TTFA at launch 575.5 / 788 ->
  568.5 / 794 ms; resolve at launch 329.5 / 429 -> 340.5 / 416 (all of
  it the /player request, which is sent unchanged; 0 hedges in 96 new
  plays); replay TTFA 167.5 / 511 -> 181.5 / 711, per round +55, +0.5,
  +1 and -17 ms, with the app's own resolved-to-open step 14-16 ms in
  every arm; replay resolve 34 / 44 -> 35 / 46. Unchanged within the
  run-to-run spread. One real googlevideo 403 (B9 arm), rescued by the
  muxed stream as in B8.
- --cancel-test 3 on the Release build: a skip during a yt-dlp resolve
  holds the interface's thread at most 13 ms.

Checked: Debug build clean (qmllint); --bounds-test 22/0 (Debug and
Release), --player-client-test 16/0, --visitor-test 30/0,
--format-test 21/0, --recovery-test 20/0, --listen-test 35/0,
--scrobble-test 32/0, --cancel-test 4/0. Also ROADMAP F38 (the
picture's yt-dlp lookups are outside the queue) and a note on F07.
